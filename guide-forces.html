Forces and motion

A force is a push or pull that may change an object's speed, direction or shape. Forces have both magnitude and direction, so two forces of equal size do not always produce the same effect if their directions differ.

Balanced and unbalanced forces

When the overall (resultant) force on an object is zero, its velocity does not change. This means an object can remain at rest or continue moving in a straight line at constant speed. A nonzero resultant force causes acceleration, which can include changing speed or direction.

Worked example

One person pushes a box to the right with a force of 20 N while friction acts left with 8 N. The resultant horizontal force is 12 N to the right. This does not by itself tell you the speed; it tells you the direction of the horizontal acceleration, assuming these are the only horizontal forces.

Friction and resistance

Friction acts to oppose relative motion or the tendency of surfaces to slide. Air resistance is a drag force produced when an object moves relative to air. Resistance can help, as when shoes grip the ground, or hinder, as when it slows a moving bicycle.

Weight and mass

Mass measures how much matter an object contains and is measured in kilograms. Weight is the gravitational force on the object and is measured in newtons. Their numerical relationship depends on gravitational field strength.

Check your understanding

1. A car travels along a straight road at constant speed. Must a forward resultant force act on it?

Show explanationNo. The net force is zero if the velocity is constant; the driving force can balance resistive forces.

2. Two teams pull a rope in opposite directions with forces of 100 N each. What is the resultant force?

Show explanationZero newtons, because equal forces acting in opposite directions cancel.
